Can a learned controller beat a classical one that is given the same care?

PID, computed torque with LQR, iLQR/MPPI, residual RL, PPO and imitation students (MLP, diffusion, flow) compete on seven arm tasks under one audited protocol. Most of the time they tie. Where there is headroom, it comes from something unexpected.

The question

Papers often report that learning-based controllers beat classical ones. But the classical side usually gets a textbook PID and no tuning budget. This project asks the stricter question: if the classical controller gets extended tooling on the same footing as the learner, can a learned controller still win by a margin that matters? The margin is fixed in advance: +10 percentage points of success rate.

Seven tasks, one shared arena

Every task and controller plugs into one interface (gym-like environment, composable weaknesses, protocol-v2 metrics, paired confidence intervals, video recorder), so any pair can be compared the same way. A fair protocol

  1. Classical tooling extended on the same footing. The classical side gets tuning, adaptation and estimation tools as well, not a fixed textbook gain.
  2. Three-axis budget. Training/tuning compute, data and wall-clock are counted for both sides, and over-runs are disclosed.
  3. Rules fixed before testing. Hypotheses live in time-stamped hypothesis cards with a public deviation table.
  4. Sanity gate for learners. A learner must first show it learned something on easy conditions; otherwise it cannot be used as evidence against learning.
  5. Locked evaluation sets with an access log. Calibration, holdout, fresh and locked test seed sets are disjoint; every evaluation of a locked set is logged.

Main findings

K2. No learned method beat the strongest classical baseline by the +10 pp bar in any of the 8 settings.

Only the whip has headroom. In 3 settings the residual-RL learner did not learn, so those settings cannot show that classical is better. With a single-seed-gated exploration scale, residual PPO degraded its base controller by 17.6 to 23.8 pp where it was tested.

K3. On the realistic whip, the largest rung of the headroom ladder is parameter estimation, and a large share of it is recoverable by choosing the plan more carefully.

On locked sets (n = 500), knowing the true parameters adds +17.6 pp [12.6, 22.6] (R1 - R0). Scoring candidate swings across several plausible plants recovers 0.59 [0.36, 0.82] of that gap with a classical Laplace ensemble and 0.90 [0.68, 1.16] with estimators trained on simulator labels. That the bottleneck is plan selection is an interpretation; variance reduction by averaging over nearby plants is not ruled out.

The learned side ends up slightly ahead, with a caveat.

Under the same robust plan-selection rule, the learned estimator is ahead of the upgraded classical controller by +5.6 pp [0.6, 10.6]. An advantage of 10 pp or more cannot be excluded, so the pre-specified equivalence claim is not supported. The learned method beat the original classical baseline R0 by +16.2 [11.0, 21.4]; the upgraded classical (C-ens) was built after that result was seen, and this order is disclosed in the paper.

K4 (secondary). For a multimodal teacher, canonicalising the teacher helped every student by 17 to 34 pp.

The best generative student exceeded the MLP by +5.0 pp (95% CI [-0.3, 10.2]), below the bar. This is one task, behaviour cloning only, and the student was chosen post hoc.

Honest negative results

  • RL did not learn where it mattered most. On the realistic whip, 11 PPO and 2 SAC configurations tuned on calibration seeds all stayed within -3 to +2 pp of initialisation.
  • Imitation students only match the teacher on tracking; none wins. There is no headroom by construction when the teacher is exact.
  • Order effects are disclosed. The upgraded classical baseline C-ens was added after the learned method had already beaten the weaker one, and one hypothesis rule (H16b) fails on a technicality that the paper reports as a card inconsistency.
  • Scope. One simulator, mostly one task (the whip), in-distribution learned estimators.

Can a learned controller beat a classical one that is given the same care?

PID, computed torque with LQR, iLQR/MPPI, residual RL, PPO and imitation students (MLP, diffusion, flow) compete on seven arm tasks under one audited protocol. Most of the time they tie. Where there is headroom, it comes from something unexpected.

Left: PID. Right: computed torque with LQR gains. Same arm, same figure, same disturbances (30% payload error, sensor noise, 10 ms delay, torque pulses).

The question

Papers often report that learning-based controllers beat classical ones. But the classical side usually gets a textbook PID and no tuning budget. This project asks the stricter question: if the classical controller gets extended tooling on the same footing as the learner, can a learned controller still win by a margin that matters? The margin is fixed in advance: +10 percentage points of success rate.

Seven tasks, one shared arena

Every task and controller plugs into one interface (gym-like environment, composable weaknesses, protocol-v2 metrics, paired confidence intervals, video recorder), so any pair can be compared the same way. The clips below show the classical teacher on calibration seeds; where a second clip is shown, it is a calibration seed on which the teacher fails once a single model error is injected.

1. Writing / figure tracking

PID vs CTC+LQR
  • Goal. The tool tip draws a glyph (S, 8, M, Z, heart or circle) with minimum-jerk timing; inverse kinematics turns the figure into a joint reference, and the score is the joint-space tracking error (RMSE).
  • Classical teacher. The ladder runs PID, computed torque with LQR gains (CTC+LQR), iLQR, iLQR-MPC and MPPI. In a 5-seed pilot the RMSE was 0.0074 rad for PID and 0.0061 rad for CTC+LQR.
  • Weaknesses. Payload error, sensor noise, delay and torque pulses can be injected into the true plant while the controller keeps its nominal model.
  • Role in the study. This is the S1–S3 setting. It has little headroom by construction: with a retuned computed-torque controller, residual PPO shows no decisive difference, and all five imitation students stay within 0.5% to 6.2% of the teacher.

2. Pole balancing on the tip

classical ladder only
  • Goal. A passive-hinge pole stands on the tool tip. Keep it upright (the episode ends when it leans beyond 0.6 rad) and the tip near a hold point; the main metric is survival time.
  • Classical teacher. LQR on the linearised pole plus operational-space computed torque. A plain position PD cannot balance a pole, so the PID baseline is a velocity servo driven by a PID on the pole angle; it balances the nominal case but is weak, which is what the ladder is meant to show.
  • Role in the study. Pole balancing was run only on the classical ladder, with no learned comparison.

3. Push-T

contact task
  • Goal. A 2-link arm pushes a T-shaped block onto a fixed goal pose. Success means the block covers at least 90% of the goal area.
  • Classical teacher. A planner-based teacher that models table friction as purely viscous, which gives an ellipsoidal limit surface as in quasi-static pushing theory.
  • Weaknesses and failure. The real table also has dry friction the teacher does not model, and the block's centre of mass is offset from what the camera-like observation shows. The clip on the right adds a sensor delay: in a pilot the teacher succeeded 19 of 20 times with 20 ms delay and 0 of 20 with 30 ms.
  • Role in the study. The nominal classical success rate is already 0.990 / 0.995 on the two evaluation sets, so a +10 percentage-point win is arithmetically impossible. Residual PPO tied it. This is the example of a classical controller that is almost unbeatable.

4. Racket juggling

mirror-law teacher
  • Goal. A 3-link arm keeps a ball bouncing with a flat racket; the score counts hits.
  • Classical teacher. A Buhler/Koditschek-style mirror law plus computed torque. The study compares learners with T-robust, the same teacher tuned with CMA-ES over the distribution of plant errors.
  • Weaknesses. In S4 one known error is injected and retuning one teacher parameter fixes it; in S5 the error is wide and unknown per episode, and the classical answer is a robust teacher tuned by CMA-ES over the error distribution.
  • Role in the study. The untuned teacher is easy to beat (+292% median hits), but a one-parameter retune also reaches 100 hits. Against the strong baseline, residual PPO degrades its base, losing 23.8 percentage points on the HOLD set.

5. Catching a thrown ball

intercept teacher
  • Goal. A 3-DoF arm with a shallow basket must catch a ball thrown into the workspace and keep it inside the basket for at least 0.5 s.
  • Classical teacher. It estimates the ball's velocity from positions only and intercepts using a ballistic prediction.
  • Weaknesses and failure. Delaying the ball observation by 10 ms is enough to break the prediction: in a pilot the teacher caught 19 of 20 balls without error but only 6 of 20 with the delay.
  • Role in the study. With the strongest classical settings the teacher reaches 0.992 / 1.000 success, so the residual-PPO comparison is a tie at the ceiling. The task adds breadth but no evidence for the main hypothesis.

6. Whip / rope fling

under-actuated
  • Goal. A 3-link arm flings a free-swinging chain of ten capsules so that the chain tip passes within 0.08 m of a target outside the arm's 1.0 m reach, within 2.5 s. The chain state is not observed.
  • Classical teacher. A plan from CMA-ES keyframes tracked by computed torque, with online system identification from a short probe (T-adapt).
  • Weaknesses and failure. The planner assumes a chain length, mass and damping. In a pilot with a chain 10% shorter it succeeded 12 of 20 times. Under realistic conditions the fitted-parameter pipeline scored 65.6% against 89.2% for an oracle.
  • Role in the study. This is the one task with headroom. On the headroom ladder, getting the true plant parameters is the largest single rung (about +18 percentage points), and learned estimators recover only part of it.

7. Toss into a basket

ballistic inversion + CTC
  • Goal. The arm holds an object and must throw it into a basket placed 0.7–1.8 m away; some baskets are beyond the arm's 1.0 m reach, so the object has to be thrown.
  • Classical teacher. It searches release point, launch angle and release orientation, solves the launch speed against a drag-ballistic flight, and tracks the planned trajectory with computed torque.
  • Weaknesses and failure. The release time is scheduled by the task. If it comes 40 ms late, the object leaves the tip at the wrong place and velocity and misses the basket. Drag and object mass can also be wrong.
  • Role in the study. Classical success is about 0.68, so there is room, but the sanity gate for residual PPO failed four times (best +1.3 percentage points). The result is therefore reported as no verdict, not as a loss for learning.

A fair protocol

  1. Classical tooling extended on the same footing. The classical side gets tuning, adaptation and estimation tools as well, not a fixed textbook gain.
  2. Three-axis budget. Training/tuning compute, data and wall-clock are counted for both sides, and over-runs are disclosed.
  3. Rules fixed before testing. Hypotheses live in time-stamped hypothesis cards with a public deviation table.
  4. Sanity gate for learners. A learner must first show it learned something on easy conditions; otherwise it cannot be used as evidence against learning.
  5. Locked evaluation sets with an access log. Calibration, holdout, fresh and locked test seed sets are disjoint; every evaluation of a locked set is logged.

Main findings

K2. No learned method beat the strongest classical baseline by the +10 pp bar in any of the 8 settings.

Only the whip has headroom. In 3 settings the residual-RL learner did not learn, so those settings cannot show that classical is better. With a single-seed-gated exploration scale, residual PPO degraded its base controller by 17.6 to 23.8 pp where it was tested.

K3. On the realistic whip, the largest rung of the headroom ladder is parameter estimation, and a large share of it is recoverable by choosing the plan more carefully.

On locked sets (n = 500), knowing the true parameters adds +17.6 pp [12.6, 22.6] (R1 - R0). Scoring candidate swings across several plausible plants recovers 0.59 [0.36, 0.82] of that gap with a classical Laplace ensemble and 0.90 [0.68, 1.16] with estimators trained on simulator labels. That the bottleneck is plan selection is an interpretation; variance reduction by averaging over nearby plants is not ruled out.

The learned side ends up slightly ahead, with a caveat.

Under the same robust plan-selection rule, the learned estimator is ahead of the upgraded classical controller by +5.6 pp [0.6, 10.6]. An advantage of 10 pp or more cannot be excluded, so the pre-specified equivalence claim is not supported. The learned method beat the original classical baseline R0 by +16.2 [11.0, 21.4]; the upgraded classical (C-ens) was built after that result was seen, and this order is disclosed in the paper.

K4 (secondary). For a multimodal teacher, canonicalising the teacher helped every student by 17 to 34 pp.

The best generative student exceeded the MLP by +5.0 pp (95% CI [-0.3, 10.2]), below the bar. This is one task, behaviour cloning only, and the student was chosen post hoc.

Honest negative results

  • RL did not learn where it mattered most. On the realistic whip, 11 PPO and 2 SAC configurations tuned on calibration seeds all stayed within -3 to +2 pp of initialisation.
  • Imitation students only match the teacher on tracking; none wins. There is no headroom by construction when the teacher is exact.
  • Order effects are disclosed. The upgraded classical baseline C-ens was added after the learned method had already beaten the weaker one, and one hypothesis rule (H16b) fails on a technicality that the paper reports as a card inconsistency.
  • Scope. One simulator, mostly one task (the whip), in-distribution learned estimators.

Try it yourself

The interactive arena runs MuJoCo in your browser (WebAssembly). All seven tasks are playable: pick a task, tune the classical teacher, inject a model error or latency, and disturb the arm, the ball or the T block with your mouse. Each task was checked against the Python version on the same seeds (validation notes). Learned controllers are not in the page yet (planned: ONNX export).

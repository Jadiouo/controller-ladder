## 1. Abstract draft (<= 200 words; 198 counted by wc -w; v4)

Learning-based controllers are often reported to beat classical ones, yet comparisons rarely give the classical side extended tooling, a disclosed budget, and rules fixed before testing. We propose an audited protocol for classical-versus-learned arm control in simulation: extended classical tooling, three-axis budgets, rules pre-specified in time-stamped hypothesis cards, a sanity gate for learners, and an access log. Across 8 tracking, juggling and whip settings, no learned method beat the strongest classical baseline by the pre-specified +10 pp bar, but only the whip has headroom; in 3 settings the residual-RL learner did not learn, and with a single-seed-gated exploration scale it degraded the base by 17.6-23.8 pp where tested. On realistic whip, locked sets (n=500) show true parameters add +17.6 pp [12.6, 22.6], yet most of that gap is recovered without better parameters: scoring candidate swings across several plausible plants, a rule built first for the learned side and then given to the classical side, recovers 0.59 [0.36, 0.82] of it with a classical Laplace ensemble and 0.90 [0.68, 1.16] with estimators trained on simulator labels. Under this rule the learned side is ahead by +5.6 pp [0.6, 10.6]; 10 pp cannot be excluded. One simulator, mostly one task.

### 3.1 Introduction [0.6 p]
- Hook: the claim "learning beats classical" is usually made against an untuned or fixed-gain baseline. Our own first result illustrates it: residual PPO beat the untuned CTC teacher by 16.7% (clean) and 44.8% (disturbed) RMSE [C-B1a], but a 2-parameter retune of the teacher removed the difference (C1 vs RL: -7.1% [CI touches 0]) [C-B1b].
- Question: what extra ingredient does a student need? Candidate ingredients: more information (privileged state, oracle), better optimisation (RL fine-tuning, DAgger), better model class (generative students), more data.
- Contributions K1-K4 (one sentence each, with the single most important number).
- Scope sentence: "to our knowledge, no prior work combines ... (search limited to 2026-10; see Related Work)". Do not write "first".

### 3.2 Related work [0.4 p]
All entries below checked against arXiv text on 2026-11-03 (`citations_check.md`, `related_check.md`).
- Classical-vs-RL comparisons and fairness: OSCAR (Wong et al., arXiv 2110.00704; learned-dynamics operational-space controller vs OSC, IK and joint-space RL policies on a Franka Panda, all trained with PPO), Leveling the Playing Field (2506.17832, RSS 2025; the closest in spirit), Real Robot Challenge (Bauer et al., 2109.10957; classical methods won the 2020 phase, RL the 2021 phase), Aljalbout et al. action space (2312.03673; over 250 RL agents, 13 control spaces; RL only).
- Distilling MPC/robust MPC into networks: Nubert et al. 1912.10360; Tagliabue and How 2306.00286 (multirotor); Diffusion-Based Approximate MPC 2504.04603 (Marquez Julbe et al.; 7-DoF arm, kinematic simulation; student has a higher success rate than the MPC teacher but a larger end-of-run error; no model-error, perturbation or payload test).
- Closest prior comparisons (M11 closed; full text read and numbers re-checked against the PDFs on 2026-11-03, `citations_check.md`): Kunapuli et al. (RSS 2025, 2506.17832) show that the gap between a quadrotor RL tracker and a geometric controller is much smaller than previously published once objective, tuning data and feedforward are equalised (geometric control has lower steady-state error, RL better transient); no imitation learner. Samuel and Verma (2026, 2609.29424) compare PID, LQR, MPC, SAC and a BC+DAgger-distilled MPC student on a hybrid rigid-pneumatic arm, in a simulator that is the controllers' own model (with a mass/inertia mismatch test of +20 % and +40 % in which the student is no more robust than its teacher), with an RL baseline that stalls (SAC+HER 74.2 % at N=1, 46.8 % at N=2) and no pre-specified protocol reported; the student does not beat the teacher (92.4 vs 95.0 % at N=2 on identical goals; 94.2 % vs 99.7 % at N=3). Novelty is therefore narrowed: not 'the first three-way comparison on one arm', but an independent-physics manipulator, an auditable fairness protocol (three-axis budgets, sanity gate, time-stamped cards, locked-set log), extended classical tooling, and the headroom ladder.
- Residual learning: Silver et al. 1812.06298; Johannink et al. 1812.03201; ResiP 2407.16677 (Ankile et al.; frozen Diffusion-Policy base plus RL residual; in the appendix an MLP base loses success under exploration noise, converges to a similar performance on the easier task and collapses on the higher-randomness task; do not cite as "diffusion base is better").
- Generative policies: Much Ado About Noising 2512.01809 (Pan et al., ICLR 2026; generative control policies do not owe their success to multi-modality, the advantage comes from iterative computation with supervised intermediate steps; a two-step regression policy, MIP, essentially matches flow policies); one-step flow (OFP 2603.12480, Li et al., arXiv only; CoLA-Flow 2601.23087, Wu et al.; speed claims such as "over 100x" are author-reported, abstract level).
- Differentiation: our focus is the protocol (extended classical tooling, disclosed budgets, sanity gate, audit log) and a headroom decomposition, not a new method.

### 3.5 Discussion and answer to the main question [0.3 p]
Proposed answer (inference, hedged): under this protocol a student needs (i) headroom the classical toolbox cannot reach, which exists only in realistic whip (oracle gap +27.4 pp on TEST-C); (ii) a learner that passes the sanity gate (PPO and SAC did not on whip or juggling; with a single-seed-gate exploration scale PPO degrades S5 and S6 by 23.8 and 17.6 pp); (iii) a planning step that does not trust one estimate: scoring candidates across several plausible plants recovers 0.59 [0.36, 0.82] of the R1 - R0 gap (classical Laplace ensemble) and 0.90 [0.68, 1.16] (learned estimators), with the learned side ahead by +5.6 pp [0.6, 10.6]; 10 pp is not excluded. An untested alternative is variance reduction: averaging the model score over four nearby plants may smooth a success rate that is near-chaotic in theta_hat. Counter-evidence: under actuator noise x4 the classical T-adapt3-full lost 12.5 pp and the learned estimator did not (-16.5 pp [-28.5, -5.0], HOLD n=200; `s13b/README.md:76`). We found no control-level learner that beats the strongest classical controller. Full text: Appendix A.1.

## 5. Limitations paragraph (main text, <= 250 words; v4)

**Limitations.** (1) *RL did not learn where it matters.* On realistic whip, 13 tuned PPO/SAC configurations and a 12M-step run stayed within -3 to +2 pp of initialisation; the sanity gate passed only marginally and later gates are single-seed. Our null is "not learned under this recipe", not "cannot learn". (2) *Holdout reuse.* HOLD selected the best classical and was then reused, so classical baselines are optimistic there (R0 69.2% on HOLD vs 65.6-66.4% on the three locked sets). (3) *Our own realism.* Noise, friction and mass magnitudes were set by us, not derived from hardware. (4) *One simulator, one dominant task.* All results are MuJoCo; the mechanistic claims rest on whip alone. (5) *Budgets and multiplicity.* Budget ratios were unequal (up to 9-17x); Holm applies within a hypothesis only; learned advantage +5.6 pp [0.6, 10.6] does not exclude 10 pp. Details: Appendix A.2.

### A.1 Section 3.5 full text (moved from Discussion, v3 wording; R2-M8a citation fixed, R2-M2 caveat added in the short version)
Proposed answer (inference, hedged; v3): under this protocol, in these settings, a student needs, in order: (i) headroom the classical toolbox cannot reach (exists only in realistic whip: oracle gap +23.6 pp on TEST-B, +27.4 on TEST-C); (ii) a learner that passes the sanity gate on that gap at the control level (PPO/SAC did not on whip or juggling; with the single-seed-gate exploration scale PPO degrades S5/S6 by 23.8 and 17.6 pp); (iii) information precision high enough for the planner, **and, found in TEST-D, a planning step that does not trust one estimate**: scoring candidates across several plausible plants takes a classical Laplace ensemble to 0.59 [0.36, 0.82] of the gap and a learned-estimator ensemble to 0.90 [0.68, 1.16]. So the extra ingredient that helped was a *decision rule over an uncertainty set* (usable by either side), plus a small advantage for learned estimators with simulator labels; we did not find a case where a control-level learner (PPO, SAC, imitation student) beats the strongest classical controller. We have not shown that control-level learning is unattainable.
Also: what the null does not say; implications for benchmark design (always report the gate and the oracle).
Counter-evidence in the main text (M9): under actuator noise x4 the classical T-adapt3-full lost 12.5 pp while the learned estimator did not (paired difference -16.5 pp [-28.5, -5.0], HOLD seeds n=200, frozen methods, no re-tuning at higher noise; App C, s13b/table.md:66-80 (drops) and s13b/README.md:76 (the -16.5 difference)). This is weak evidence (HOLD only), but together with the locked-set learned advantage in H16a (+5.6 [0.6, 10.6]) it points against a blanket "classical is at least as good" and is reported here rather than only in the appendix. Cost caveat: the classical ensemble needs about 3x the CPU time per episode (63.3 s vs 20.4 s), and the learned estimators needed 13,120 training episode-equivalents with simulator labels (9-17x the classical budget, C-D1).

### A.2 Limitations, full text (moved from Section 5; items (1)-(5) are condensed in the main text; (12) updated for spike15f, (13) added for R2-M1)
**Limitations.** (1) *RL did not learn in the settings that matter most.* In the realistic whip, 11 PPO and 2 SAC configurations tuned on CAL, 3 seeds of 3M steps, and a 12M-step run all stayed within -3 to +2 pp of initialisation (s13a/table.md:51-53); in idealised whip all six PPO runs were flagged "not learned" (s09b/table.md:78-83); in juggling the residual stayed at 0.15-0.18 Nm of a 12 Nm cap and 4 of 6 runs chose the first checkpoint (s07/README.md:50). The sanity gate was "passed marginally" and only after raising the exploration scale, with a recipe chosen among 8 gate runs, a +10.2 pp result against a +10 pp threshold, n=500 instead of the pre-specified n=200, the zero-residual <= 40% rule unmet, and SAC not passing (s13a/table.md:19-27). The S5/S6 PPO runs were re-run with the single-seed-gate exploration scale in spike15b (M4 closed): they degrade the base by 23.8 and 17.6 pp, but each gate is a single-seed shot (S5 gate easy; S6 budget 3.2x classical), the recipe was not swept on the real condition, and the spike15c asymmetric-critic gate failed (+7.2, +1.2). Our null on residual RL is therefore "not learned under about 3-12M steps, one recipe per setting and about 13 tuned configurations on whip", not "cannot learn". (2) *Holdout reuse.* HOLD (1100-1599) was used to choose best-classical in spike09b/10b and then reused as the main evaluation set in spikes 13a, 13b and 14a; the classical baseline is therefore optimistically selected on 300 of the 500 HOLD seeds (69.7% on HOLD vs 65.6% on TEST-B, s10b/table.md:123, 14). NEW seeds replicate the direction. Since v3 the ladder (TEST-C) and the robust-selection results (TEST-D) have locked-set evidence; the multimodal-teacher result (K4), the group split, the fragility sweep and the 9-network extension do not. R0 is lower on all three locked sets (65.6, 65.6, 66.4) than on HOLD (69.2), consistent with the selection optimism. (3) *Realistic conditions are our own choice.* Sensor noise 1e-3 rad / 1e-2 rad/s, actuator noise U(0.01, 0.02) Nm, Coulomb friction 2e-3 Nm x U(0.5, 1.5) and per-link mass factors exp(U(-0.3, 0.3)) were set by us (magnitudes fixed before the results, per docs/progress.md spike10a entry) but are not derived from hardware; the friction value is an estimate (spike10a/dist.json, s10b/README.md:101). (4) *One simulator.* All results are MuJoCo; no second engine, no hardware. (5) *Concentration on whip.* Five of the eight settings are tracking or juggling, where headroom is small or absent (juggling: no cell gains >= 10 pp, spike08_diag/expA.log; tracking: ties); the mechanistic claims (K3, K4) rest on the whip alone. (6) *Unequal tuning knobs; "equal tools" is not claimed.* The classical side tuned 5-13 parameters with 750-9,050 episodes and received the true generative distribution as bounds and priors; the learned side used 10^4-10^5 episodes and simulator labels (spike14a: 9-17x budget; spike13a about 7x; spike03a: classical used more simulation steps than one RL policy). Neither side is "equal" on every axis; we report the axes rather than equalise them. In the estimator duel the classical side tried only three strengthened variants (s14a/README.md:65). (7) *Forking paths.* The deviation table is long: W1/W2 strengths set after seeing TEST (spike02); hyper-parameters chosen after one TEST look (learn_demo); RL recipe repaired and checkpoint selection added after round 1 (spike06); PPO not fixed before TEST (spike07); plant step changed after CAL tuning (spike10b); dose curve run after the locked TEST-B (spike10b); post hoc analyses in spikes 13b/14a. Statistics are paired bootstrap CIs per comparison with Holm only inside a hypothesis; there is no correction across spikes, so the positive exceptions are exploratory. Git order of rule and result is verifiable only for part of the spikes (`docs/paper/prespec_audit.md`); spike04's H7 was amended after results and learn_demo was not pre-specified. (8) *Pipeline noise.* Re-running identical estimator models with float rounding changes moved holdout success by up to 3.6 pp per model (s14a/table.md:20 vs 32-37), comparable to several reported differences. (9) *Power and equivalence.* Most pre-specified tests had CI half-widths of 4-9 pp (TEST n=50 comparisons: up to about 15 pp); "no side wins at the 10 pp bar" is not an equivalence test (post hoc TOST: `docs/paper/stats/equivalence.md`; only rows whose 90% CI lies inside +-10 pp may be called equivalent), and differences of +1 to +5 pp (for example the 3-model ensemble, +4.8 [-0.8, 10.4], a post hoc hint) cannot be excluded. On the locked TEST-D, the learned advantage under robust selection is +5.6 pp with 95% CI [0.6, 10.6]: it is real by H16a (Holm p 0.032), but an advantage of 10 pp cannot be excluded (H16b not supported), and the v9 card is internally inconsistent about this bound (two-sided 95% rule vs one-sided Holm p; the rule text is applied). (10) *Fairness of the plan-selection duel.* The classical ensemble (Laplace posterior) uses the generative parameter distribution as prior, was picked on CAL among 20 variants (B among 22), takes about 3x the CPU time per episode (63.3 vs 20.4 s), and was not tuned further (no Laplace-width sensitivity run); the learned side used 13,120 training episodes with simulator labels. The multi-start classical family was degenerate (439/500 holdout episodes without a distinct second solution). Both sides are frozen by the v9 card. (11) *Pre-specified vs post hoc.* Only the cards v8, v8.1 and v9 are git-ordered before their runs; spike15b/15c/15d rules are README-only, and the 9-network extension, B9, the reverse-order ladder, S6-X1 and L-ens/C-MAP are post hoc or descriptive. (12) *Numerical warnings.* The TEST-D log has 125,555 MuJoCo 'simulation unstable' warnings (4,621 on holdout). A re-run on 100 holdout episodes (spike15f, commit a8b4895) attributes 92-98% of the warnings to planner model rollouts and none to the executed plant, with 0/100 diverged executed episodes for R0, L-ens, B and C-ens (one plant warning occurred in a non-selected offline candidate execution); the check is n=100 on holdout only, the full-n holdout and TEST-D check was not repeated.
